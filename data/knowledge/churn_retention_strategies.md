# Churn prediction and retention strategies

Churn models are valuable only if they lead to better retention decisions. The goal is to find customers
who are likely to leave and who can still be persuaded to stay, then spend the retention budget where it
has the largest effect.

Common churn drivers in subscription businesses are short tenure, flexible month-to-month contracts,
rising bills, frequent support contacts and poor service quality. Early-life customers are the most
fragile, so onboarding programmes often reduce churn more cheaply than late discounts.

Rank customers by predicted churn probability and combine it with customer value: a high-risk,
high-value customer deserves a personal call, while a high-risk, low-value customer may receive an
automated offer. Tune the decision threshold to the budget and to the cost ratio between a missed
churner (lost lifetime value) and an unnecessary offer (discount cost).

Typical actions include contract upgrades with a loyalty discount, proactive resolution for customers
with many support calls, payment-method changes that reduce friction, and bundles that increase switching
costs. Match the action to the driver explained by the model for that customer.

Measure impact with a controlled experiment: hold out a random group of at-risk customers who receive no
offer, and compare churn rates. Without a control group, it is impossible to tell whether an offer kept a
customer or was given to someone who would have stayed anyway. Retrain the model regularly, because
behaviour changes after retention campaigns.
